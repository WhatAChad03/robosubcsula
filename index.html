who we are

An interdisciplinary team, split into two hulls.

We're a student organization at California State University, Los Angeles, building autonomous submarine vehicles across two parallel efforts — a student club build and a senior design build — so more students get hands-on time regardless of where they are in the program.

Five subteams carry the work: Frame & Hull, Electronics, Controls, Autonomy, and Computer Vision. Mechanical, electrical, and computer science majors all end up welding, wiring, and writing code on the same vehicle.

subteams

Five disciplines, one hull.

Every member sits on one subteam and works directly on the physical vehicle from week one.

FH

Frame & Hull

The Frame and Hull subteam is responsible for making the design of the AUV itself. This subteam also works with SolidWorks to determine placement of other submarine components and varying approach decisions.

EL

Electronics

The Electronics subteam is focuses on the management of internal current conversion from the battery to all of the other electronic parts in the robotic submarine. The team creates schematics for the functionality of buck converters.

CT

Controls

Controls subteam creates the frameworks that will assess the sensors, the thrusters and other software components. Because the AUV is autonomous, Controls will make a GUI in order to debug the code and test the controllers.

AU

Autonomy

The Autonomy subteam is responsible for writing the state machine of the AUV. The state machine determines what the AUV is doing at any time, and it's very important when deciding what to do next.

CV

Computer Vision

The Computer Vision subteam provides the AUV ability to locate & recognize objets using YOLO to train custom object detection models for providing computer vision. They also work with the submarine's camera interface for real-time detection using a ROS node

competition

RoboSub, by RoboNation.

Every year, teams from around the world bring autonomous submarines to compete in a themed obstacle course — and this year? It's the iconic Science Fiction movie Stargate!

Our vehicle has to detect, decide, and act underwater with no remote control and no live video feed to a human operator.
